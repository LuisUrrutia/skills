# Progress and continuity

Read when revising a plan from progress, resuming it, or saving an authorized
learning record. Use the learner's actual goals and evidence, not the previous
plan's momentum.

## Resume the right context

Confirm that the plan or record belongs to this learner and topic. Read the
current objective, constraints, completed work, and unresolved difficulties.
Where identity or record ownership is unclear, ask before using another person's
history; independent planning can continue without that record.

Check whether the goal or available time changed. Reuse valid progress, but
reassess a prerequisite when current attempts contradict the saved understanding.
Preserve corrections instead of quietly treating old guesses as facts.

## Evaluate the next step

Distinguish these observations in ordinary language:

- Presented or read: the learner encountered the material.
- Self-reported: the learner says they know it or have prior experience.
- Supported application: a task succeeded with hints, examples, or corrections.
- Independent application: the learner used the idea in the observed task without
  that support.

Do not convert these into invented mastery percentages or permanent ability
labels. A later changed-context task can supply evidence of retention and transfer;
an immediate repetition does not establish either broadly.

For a difficult checkpoint, identify the specific gap, reduce or change the task,
and reinforce the prerequisite that matters. For a comfortable checkpoint, skip
redundant practice or add a useful variation. If the learner declines practice,
continue the requested explanation or plan and leave understanding unmeasured.

Revise the next milestone and its criteria using this evidence. When changing
the learner's desired outcome would be necessary, present the tradeoff rather
than silently replacing their goal. A change in pacing need not restart the course.

## Save only useful continuity

When a continuing record is requested, keep the learner or workspace identity,
topic, current objective, demonstrated or reported progress, support used,
remaining gap, and next step. Include evidence only at the detail needed to resume.
Record material corrections and dates where stale facts matter. Do not save a
transcript or sensitive personal detail simply because it was available.

Use existing files and formats when sufficient. A single plan with a short progress
section may be enough; separate mission, glossary, resource library, and numbered
records are optional structures for a need, not prerequisites to teach. Keep
private company materials within their authorized audience and destination.
