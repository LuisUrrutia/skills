# Learning plan

Design a learning progression for the intended person and purpose. Cover technical
and nontechnical subjects. Own the sequence, scope, workload, and adjustments;
`teach` owns the content and feedback of individual lessons.

## Establish the outcome

Use the request and available context to identify the learner, the outcome they
want, relevant prior knowledge, and constraints such as a deadline, study time,
budget, language, or preferred format. Do not inherit another person's profile
because they share the installation or workspace. Use an existing record only
when it clearly belongs to this learner and topic.

Express success as something the learner can explain, perform, or evaluate.
Ask for missing constraints only when they materially change the plan. If the
goal is sufficiently clear but available time is unknown, provide a milestone-based
plan with adjustable workload rather than inventing a weekly commitment. Mark
estimates and assumptions as such. An infeasible deadline needs a smaller priority
scope or an explicit tradeoff, not a promise of mastery.

Identify the deliverable: a roadmap, a schedule, a revised plan, or a complete
course with lesson content. Honor requested chapters, examples, exercises, and
diagrams. A plan request does not require writing every lesson, but a complete
course request must not end with an outline and an offer to fill it later.

## Sequence the learning

Work backward from the outcome. Order objectives by prerequisites, keeping only
the foundation needed for the next meaningful application. Build manageable
chapters or milestones. Respect explicit requests for no exercises or assessment;
use worked examples and optional self-review instead. Otherwise, include:

- A concrete objective and the prerequisite knowledge it uses.
- The concepts and worked example that develop it.
- A relevant exercise, application, or reflection task and feedback criteria.
- An observable checkpoint for deciding whether to advance or reinforce.

Include the requested diagrams or actual examples rather than placeholders that
say to add them later. Match the level of detail to the deliverable; a short
roadmap can give representative examples and practice briefs, while a course
requires the requested teaching content. Avoid turning every chapter into an
identical template when its purpose differs.

Prioritize a small number of reliable, level-appropriate resources and explain
what each contributes. Verify resource titles, availability, access requirements,
and changing factual claims before recommending them. Do not invent links or
require paid materials or communities without a need grounded in the request.
Use supplied resources when they fit; state gaps instead of pretending they cover
the entire outcome.

## Connect plans to lessons

For requested lesson content, use `teach` when available. Pass the learner's
purpose, objective, prerequisites, requested format, practice preferences, and
relevant sources. Review the returned lesson against the plan and continue through
the remaining requested chapters. Return evidence of difficulty to the plan's
adjustment step; do not restart planning after every lesson.

If `teach` is unavailable, produce the requested content directly from supported
sources and disclose any material capability limit. Neither planning nor teaching
requires installing a plugin or creating a new workspace.

Use `communicate-clearly` for prose and `write-documentation` for a saved course
when available. These owners supply presentation mechanics; this skill retains
the learning sequence and completeness of the requested deliverable.

## Review and adjust

Read [references/progress.md](references/progress.md) when reviewing progress,
resuming a saved plan, or maintaining an authorized learning record.

Build checkpoints into the plan that use an attempt, explanation, or application
to reveal the relevant gap. A completed chapter or confident self-report is not
proof of retention. Use observed difficulty to change the example, prerequisite,
workload, or next task. Do not make quizzes mandatory when the learner prefers
another way to practice or demonstrate progress.

Include opportunities to revisit important concepts across the requested span
and apply them in changed contexts. Keep the schedule proportional to available
time; a request for a calendar is not authorization to create calendar events,
reminders, enrollments, or recurring tasks.

## Deliver

Check that the sequence reaches the requested outcome, prerequisites are supplied
or stated, workload fits the known constraints, and each checkpoint has usable
criteria. For an interview plan, prioritize the role and time available without
claiming knowledge of an employer's private questions or guaranteeing success.

Deliver the plan or complete requested course and a clear starting point. State
material assumptions, source gaps, and unverified elements. Live teaching starts
when requested or already included in the task; a planning-only request ends
with the plan. Save files and progress only when requested, at the designated
destination under existing conventions, without creating a learning database by
default. Follow the active output-language rules.

## Source maintenance

For requested source checks or updates, use `agent-instructions` with `origin.txt`.
Ordinary planning does not fetch donor skills or require authoring consultations.
